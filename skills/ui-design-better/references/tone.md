# Product language

Read for UI copy, product narrative, headings, onboarding, explanations, and errors. These principles also help longer content within a product; they do not expand this skill into every unrelated writing task.

## Meaning and truth

Natural, readable, complete language comes before uniform brevity. Preserve facts, useful qualifications, attribution, and the author's actual viewpoint. Editing for polish does not authorize new experiences, feelings, customer feedback, outcomes, statistics, or endorsements.

Distinguish what was experienced, what a source documents, what is inferred, and what is planned. Verify unstable factual claims against current material when relevant. If a source figure conflicts with a calculation, check the discrepancy rather than preserving an error for fidelity. Do not improve persuasiveness by inventing a scene or consequence.

Explain what changed, how it affects the reader, and the limits supported by evidence. “Improves efficiency” is weaker than a specific supported consequence. If the consequence is unknown, say less rather than making one up.

## Information order

Start from the reader's question, their existing knowledge, and the judgments the material supports. Introduce an unfamiliar product, object, feature, term, or metric when it first becomes necessary. Supply the missing step, without explaining what the audience already knows. Do not define one unfamiliar term through another unexplained term.

Make actors, actions, objects, causes, results, conditions, and limits understandable. A grammatical sentence may still fail to connect to its paragraph. Repair information order or the missing relationship before adding a transition word such as “therefore.” A heading does not replace the explanation needed in the body.

Use connected paragraphs for explanation and causality; lists for genuinely parallel items or steps; tables for comparison and retrieval. Do not turn all prose into short line fragments to simulate rhythm. The format should reveal the understanding sequence rather than determine it.

Headings name actual content, action, or a supported judgment. Claims of ownership, timing, number, and result need evidence. Avoid manufacturing drama with a colon, a rhetorical question, or “not X, but Y” when no meaningful distinction exists. These forms remain useful when the argument actually requires them.

Explain the main point without making readers open a link. Place a useful source or destination beside the associated statement; links support meaning rather than replace it or accumulate in an unrelated pile.

## Voice and sentence form

Use everyday verbs when accurate, with a concrete object: what is checked, changed, saved, or sent? Replace vague actions such as “get it sorted” with the actual operation when the reader otherwise has to guess. A clear subject can be omitted in context; restore it when the reader must reread to identify the actor.

In Chinese, preserve grammatical markers and particles when they express relationships, aspect, emphasis, or natural cadence. Read the original and revised wording in context before deciding whether to change them. Use natural Chinese punctuation and readable spacing between Chinese text, Latin text, and numbers where appropriate. Preserve code, exact names, URLs, and quotations.

In English, prefer ordinary direct wording and complete relationships. Formal writing can remain natural; conversational writing need not be careless. Oral and social content may use a looser cadence. Remove repeated starts and empty verbal tics when they add no meaning.

Pronouns depend on the speaker: “I” for supported personal experience/judgment, “we” for a real team or a shared walkthrough, and direct address for the reader. Do not invent a common experience or change speaker repeatedly. Do not flatten an evidenced personal judgment into generic product marketing just to sound neutral.

Respect the requested edit depth. For polish, keep the underlying facts, conclusions, numbers, quotations, names, links, and necessary format. For a confusing full text, reorganize as needed without changing its conclusion or filling evidence gaps with invention. Preserve the user's chosen brand voice, humor, poetry, technical language, or deliberate repetition when they serve the work.

## UI copy as an interaction

- Buttons communicate the action and, where useful, its object or scope: “Save draft” is more informative than a repeated “Start now.”
- Labels identify the object or setting. Helper text supplies a constraint or example the user needs; it does not narrate obvious UI.
- Status explains what is happening now. A busy indicator must not claim progress or success unsupported by the system.
- Errors say what failed, where, what is retained, and a useful next step. Avoid blame and empty reassurance.
- Completion states report the actual outcome and next action. Do not say “sent” for a local animation or “saved” before confirmation.
- A short name, unit, qualifier, or status may be essential. Do not remove it just to make a screenshot cleaner.

Keep production notes, fake-success disclosures, asset provenance, and implementation explanations in the handoff unless product users need them to understand the real behavior. An honest demo notice is appropriate when interaction would otherwise mislead; a visually clean mockup does not authorize deception.

## Expressions to review in context

These are warning signals, not a banned-word filter:

| Pattern | What to examine |
| --- | --- |
| Empowerment jargon and vague abstractions, such as “empower teams” or “deliver value” | Is a concrete action or consequence missing? |
| Stock openings about a changing era or continuous development | Does the background explain this particular case? |
| “The real key,” “worth noting,” indisputable-importance claims | Is there an actual qualification, contrast, or evidence? |
| “Marks a milestone,” “lays a foundation,” “demonstrates value” | What specific supported change occurred? |
| “Experts say,” “many users report,” “research shows” | Is the source identified and actually available? |
| Automatic “thereby ensuring” or deeper-meaning conclusions | Is the causality established? |
| Generic optimism despite challenges, a new chapter, elevated endings | Is there new information, a real limit, or a concrete next step? |
| Chat greetings, praise, offers of further help inside finished copy | Is conversation residue being mistaken for the deliverable? |
| Stacked hedges such as “possibly, perhaps, to some extent” | Can one accurate uncertainty statement carry the meaning? |

Metaphor, a surprising line, a poetic heading, or an enthusiastic voice can be excellent when specific to the product and understandable. Naturalness is not achieved by stripping away all personality.

## Final reading

Read headings and paragraph openings to check the argument's path. Read the body continuously to check evidence, causes, and links between ideas. Read aloud at a natural pace to catch stiff compression, unclear references, missing objects, awkward Chinese particles, and accidental repetition. Check UI text in its actual state and viewport too.

A lint tool may flag patterns worth review; it cannot establish truth, fluency, or clarity. No warning is proof of good writing, and a matched phrase is not automatically wrong. Decide from the whole passage and the reader's task.
